AI fact-checking you can trust when it speaks

An AI that knows when its own answer is worth checking, and when to say “I don’t know”.

Language models answer confidently even when they are guessing. Know-When-To-Check wraps a frozen model in a small learned controller. For every claim it decides whether to answer, look the evidence up first, or decline. Every verdict comes with the sentence it rests on and an honest probability of being right.

1

Read

The model sees a claim and the top-3 retrieved abstracts.

2

Ask twice

Two independent answers. How much do they agree, how sure, is the evidence enough?

3

Decide

Answer, check first, or decline, whichever has the highest expected reward.

4

Check

Search and read the library, quote a sentence, and let a blind judge re-read it.

5

Verdict

Supported, refuted or not enough evidence, with its source and a calibrated credence.

Three rules make checking actually help

Without them, looking things up made the models worse: they talked themselves into confident verdicts from loosely related text.

Rule 1

No quote, no verdict

A “supported” or “refuted” must cite a sentence the agent actually read. Otherwise the honest answer is “not enough evidence”.

Rule 2

A blind judge

A separate call sees only the claim and the quoted sentence, with no search story to anchor on, and decides what it says.

Rule 3

A check is evidence, not an oracle

After checking, a second decision weighs the new verdict against the first: commit, keep the prior, or decline.

Two ways to learn the decision: credence-based vs. reinforcement learning

Same frozen model, same signals, same rules and reward. The credence-based agent fits logistic regressions for P(correct) and takes the highest expected reward. The RL agent learns a two-stage softmax policy with REINFORCE on the saved runs.
